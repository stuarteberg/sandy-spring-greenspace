What is a Conservation Easement?

A powerful tool for permanently protecting your land.

Understanding Conservation Easements

A conservation easement is a voluntary legal agreement between a landowner and a land trust (like GSSGS) or government agency that permanently limits uses of the land in order to protect its conservation values.

When you donate a conservation easement, you continue to own and use your land, and you can sell it or pass it on to heirs. However, the easement permanently limits development on the property to protect its natural features.

Key Points About Conservation Easements

  • You retain ownership - The land remains yours to use, sell, or bequeath
  • Permanent protection - The easement stays with the land forever, regardless of future owners
  • Tailored to your property - Each easement is individually crafted to address the specific conservation values and your needs
  • Tax benefits - You may qualify for federal and state income tax deductions, as well as estate tax benefits

How It Works

  1. Initial Consultation

    We meet with you to discuss your goals and evaluate your property's conservation values.

  2. Property Assessment

    We document the property's natural features, boundaries, and current condition.

  3. Easement Drafting

    Working with you, we create an easement document tailored to your property and wishes.

  4. Appraisal & Tax Benefits

    A qualified appraiser determines the easement's value for tax purposes.

  5. Recording

    The easement is recorded with the county, making it part of the property's title.

  6. Ongoing Stewardship

    We monitor the property annually to ensure the easement terms are upheld.

What Can You Still Do With Your Land?

Conservation easements are flexible and can be tailored to your needs. Depending on the terms, you may be able to:

  • Continue living on and using your property
  • Farm or manage timber
  • Hunt, fish, and enjoy recreational activities
  • Build limited additional structures in designated areas
  • Sell or transfer the property

Tax Benefits

Donating a conservation easement may provide significant tax advantages:

Federal Income Tax Deduction

The value of the donated easement (the difference between the property's value before and after the easement) may be deducted as a charitable contribution. You can deduct up to 50% of your adjusted gross income per year, with a 15-year carryforward for any unused deduction.

Maryland State Tax Credit

Maryland offers a state income tax credit for donated easements, which can be used to reduce your state tax liability or, in some cases, sold to other Maryland taxpayers.

Estate Tax Benefits

By reducing the property's development value, an easement can significantly lower estate taxes, making it easier to pass the land on to the next generation.

Note: Tax laws are complex and change over time. We strongly recommend consulting with a qualified tax advisor or attorney to understand how conservation easements might benefit your specific situation.
